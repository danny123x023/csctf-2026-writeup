Transmission

Digit@l F0rtress entered the Chronos Security CTF with one goal: be known by the end of the weekend. The first flag came at 13:02 UTC on November 6, and the board started to fill.

By the close of day one we had 29 challenges solved, and the team moved like it had been working together for years. Then came the heavy hitters. Five challenges worth 540 points each fell to us, and forensics held the line with 11 solves when nothing else would.

The organizers did not make it easy. Decoy flag formats, anti-pattern traps, and contradictory evidence were everywhere. The flagship 900-point forensics challenge, Operation Blackout, ended with no solves. The teams that kept their heads were the ones that checked every clue against the rules instead of trusting the first answer that looked right.

We finished with 9 more solves on the morning of November 8, the last one at 10:26 UTC. Final standing: 212th out of 1,011 registered teams.

Lessons Learned

01 · Check every hidden place before deciding something doesn’t exist. Channel settings, metadata, and unusual file structures all held clues.
02 · Verify every intermediate result. Several solves came down to a single wrong byte or bit, so confirm each step before building on it.
03 · Read format requirements closely. Decoy flag formats and traps were part of the design, and the correct format was the one the rules described.
04 · Recognize dead ends. When an approach stops producing new information, step back and try a different angle.
